# Prototype: what shape should this take

The person sees genuinely different arrangements of the same thing and says
which is right, or more usefully, that they want the top of one and the bottom of
another.

## How many

Three by default. That is enough to show a pattern without turning a decision
into an examination. Build more when they ask for more, and say plainly that
past about five they stop differing in ways anybody can judge.

## What has to differ

Layout and order. What sits at the top, what is grouped with what, what is one
screen and what is two, what is hidden until asked for. Those are the things a
person can react to.

Not colour, not wording, not spacing. Three versions of the same arrangement in
different shades teach nothing, and they read as a real choice, so the person
spends their attention on a decision that was never open.

## Where to show them

Inside the real page, with its real surroundings and the amount of data it
really carries, wherever such a page exists. An arrangement on an empty screen
of its own always looks fine, because there is nothing around it to be wrong
against. The same arrangement dropped into the real page, under the real
navigation, next to everything else competing for the same space, often is.

Give them a way to move between the versions that they find without being told:
in view, where they are looking, not behind anything they have to be shown. That
control is not part of what they are judging, so it may look like exactly what it
is. The arrangements themselves still never announce that they are prototypes.

## What to bring back

Which arrangement was chosen, and what the production build must keep. Where
they wanted parts of two, write down which parts, because that combination is
the decision and it exists in no single version they saw.
